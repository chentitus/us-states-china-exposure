China and Taiwan side by side

Colours: China = red, Taiwan = blue. The scatter plot defaults to X = China exposure total score and Y = Taiwan exposure total score (the dashed line is 45°: states above it are more exposed to Taiwan than to China). The total and the three sub-scores for both partners can be chosen as X / Y / map variables regardless of the "Trading partner" switch. The "Trading partner" switch changes which partner the score is computed for (dependence on China or on Taiwan). The map, scatter and table also offer the China/Taiwan export ratio (exports to China ÷ exports to Taiwan) and the China/Taiwan import ratio (imports from China ÷ imports from Taiwan): above 1 means the state trades more with China than with Taiwan, 1 is parity, below 1 means Taiwan matters more (log scale on the map and scatter). Each state's detail page shows annual values, shares and the relative-dependence ratio for China and Taiwan side by side, and the top-10 products can be switched between partners.

Indicators and scores

Export dependence: exports to China (Taiwan) / state GDP, exports to China (Taiwan) / total state exports; optional services exports to China / GDP (top 10 states only). Import dependence: imports from China (Taiwan) / state GDP, imports from China (Taiwan) / total state imports. Vulnerability: share of the largest HS4 product in exports to China (Taiwan), HS4 concentration (HHI), optional share of exports hit by China's retaliatory tariffs (top 10 states only) — exposure concentrated in a few products or firms that can be targeted (for example, 94% of New Mexico's exports to China are Intel processors and 57% of North Carolina's are one drug maker's biologics). Assets and other (not counted by default): acres of farmland held by Chinese investors. For each concept the sub-score is the mean percentile of the selected indicators across states; the total is the mean of the sub-scores. Concepts are weighted equally and no single indicator is weighted. The base year can be switched; 2026 is YTD.

How to read it

  • This ranks exposure, i.e. how much China matters to a state's economy. States with high export dependence (farm, energy, semiconductor and aerospace states) lose most from Chinese retaliatory tariffs; states with high import dependence (consumer goods and electronics-assembly supply chains) face the largest cost increases from U.S. tariffs. The two act on state politics in different directions, so they are shown separately.
  • States with high vulnerability (a single HS4 product above 30% of exports to China, high HHI) face the greatest risk that one product or one firm is targeted (soybeans, crude oil, LNG, aircraft, chips, biologics); high dependence with low vulnerability (California, Texas) means the exposure is spread out.
  • Origin-of-movement state ≠ producing state: much of Louisiana's soybeans and Texas's crude oil / LNG come from the Midwest and other producing regions; compare with USCBC's "jobs supported" or USDA's state agricultural export estimates.

Limitations

  • There are no official state-level statistics on services exports; USCBC publishes estimates for the top 10 states only.
  • Chinese FDI in the U.S. by state: BEA's state-level tables list only Canada, France, Germany, Japan, the Netherlands, Switzerland and the UK, with China folded into "other"; the Rhodium US-China Investment Hub ends in 2020. Not included in this version.
  • The U.S.–China tariff war from 2025 makes 2025–2026 values volatile; choose 2024 as the base year to see the pre-tariff structure.
