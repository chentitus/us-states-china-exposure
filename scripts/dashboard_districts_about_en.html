Two measures

USCBC / Trade Partnership Worldwide estimates (source: US-China Business Council, US Exports to China 2025, one-page PDFs for each state and congressional district: 51 state pages plus 429 district pages, with the 7 single-district states represented by their state page): goods exports to China (2024), services exports (2023), jobs supported (direct plus indirect, 2023), China's rank and share among the district's export markets, and the top five goods / services exported to China. Method: state figures are allocated to 119th-Congress districts by industry using Census, USDA, BEA and Moody's data; farm products are assigned to where they are grown, not to the district of the export port. Totals (the charts are images) are taken from the China row of the "export markets" list.

Shift-share allocation (this project): exposureg(y) = Σi [ Lg,i / Li ] × Xi(y). i is a 4-digit NAICS industry; for counties, L is BLS QCEW 2024 annual average employment (county × NAICS4, private, including farm employees, so farm exports such as oilseeds and grains can be allocated; coverage 96%), with cells suppressed under QCEW disclosure rules (58%) filled with CBP 2023 noise-infused values; for districts, L is ZBP 2023 establishments by ZIP × NAICS4 × national average employment per establishment, allocated to 119th-Congress districts by each ZCTA's land-area share (Census rel2020 cd119–ZCTA relationship file; ZIPs are far smaller than counties, which greatly reduces the problem of splitting metro counties; 98% of ZIP employment is matched). Weights are normalised by the sum over areas, so each industry's national value is fully allocated. X is national exports to or imports from China (Taiwan) (Census statenaics endpoint, annual 2020–2025 and January–September 2026). County values are also divided by BEA county GDP (CAGDP2; 2024 is reused after 2024). The first version (CBP county employment plus county-to-district area allocation) is kept in *_v1_cbp_area.csv. This is structurally the same exposure definition as Autor–Dorn–Hanson's "China shock", but in levels rather than changes.

District pages also show the China/Taiwan export ratio (shift-share exports to China ÷ exports to Taiwan) and the China/Taiwan import ratio: >1 means the district's industry mix trades more with China than with Taiwan, <1 means Taiwan matters more; the map uses a red–grey–blue diverging scale (1× is grey). Both come from the same shift-share allocation, so the ratio reflects the relative exposure of the industry mix and does not depend on the size of the area.

How to read it

  • Both measures are structural estimates, not observed trade flows; official U.S. trade statistics go down only to states and metro areas (exports), not to counties or districts.
  • USCBC uses detailed-industry (Moody's) output and farm-production data; shift-share uses employment only, so it understates places with capital-intensive industries (refining, chemicals, fabs: exports per worker far above the national average) and overstates places with high employment but low export intensity. At the district level in 2024 the two have a Spearman correlation of about 0.37 and a median ratio of 1.14; they are related but not equal, and the validation scatter shows the direction of the systematic bias.
  • At the county level, "exports to China as % of GDP" can be driven up in small counties by a single plant (for example a semiconductor packaging plant or a car plant); read it together with employment and the contributing-industries table.
  • State-level check: county shift-share totals are compared with Census origin-of-movement values; port states (Louisiana, Texas) show observed values above the allocated ones, which reflects the origin-of-movement definition rather than an allocation error.

Limitations

  • 58% of QCEW county × industry cells are suppressed under disclosure rules and are filled with CBP 2023 noise-infused values; since 2018 ZBP no longer publishes ZIP-level employment, only establishment counts, so ZIP employment is estimated as "establishments × national average employment per establishment"; self-employment is excluded.
  • NAICS trade categories cover goods only (11, 21, 31–33 and special categories); services are outside the shift-share; USCBC services exports end in 2023.
  • 119th-Congress district boundaries: USCBC notes that a few redrawn districts without new ZIP assignments are missing; this project's ZCTA→district relationship file is the 119th-Congress version, so the two are consistent.
  • The USCBC 2026 edition (2025 goods data) has one-page state reports only, with no district pages.
